## Observations grounded in what was inspected

1. **Citation format.** JRAP's suggested citation is author–date with Chicago punctuation: "Hicks, Michael J. 2024. 'Federal Fiscal Exposure of US Counties.' *Journal of Regional Analysis & Policy* 54 (2): 36–48." The draft uses biblatex-chicago (author–date), which produces this form.
2. **Keywords.** Published articles show short, plain keyword phrases (e.g. "transfers", "effective tax rate"). The draft's five keywords follow that pattern.
3. **Editorial stance (official pages).** The journal wants work that is "theory-based, empirically sound, and policy-relevant" and that builds on the regional science / regional economics literature. It encourages junior scholars. Regional fiscal topics (county-level federal transfers) fit its scope, as the 2024 Presidential Symposium issue shows.

## How these shaped the revision (editorial judgment, not copied from JRAP articles)

No prose from JRAP articles was read or reused.

- **Opening.** The paper now opens with the allocation tension: federal funds become local investment only through a local application. The distributional consequences come next, then the regional question of why smaller jurisdictions apply less.
- **Regional variation.** The paper now presents the decile gradient (Figure 2) and the maps (Figure 1) as evidence that the disparity runs across the whole county-size distribution rather than affecting a few places.
- **Contribution.** There are three contributions, each tied to a regional or intergovernmental-finance question. No novelty superlatives are used.
- **Methods and limits.** A subsection titled "What the Design Identifies" states plainly what is descriptive and what is causal. Limitations get their own subsection.
- **Policy.** Implications are framed as consequences for grant design, and the text says explicitly that the options were not evaluated.
