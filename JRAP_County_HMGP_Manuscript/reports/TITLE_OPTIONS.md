# Title options

**Original:** "Declared but Not Applied: County Size and Participation in Post-Disaster Hazard Mitigation after Tornadoes and Severe Storms"

The main title is memorable and states the central finding: a declaration happened, and no application followed. The subtitle reads as hazard management ("participation in post-disaster hazard mitigation") rather than as a question about access to public funds. It makes no causal claim.

## Alternatives

1. **"Declared but Not Applied: County Size and Access to Federal Hazard Mitigation Grants after Tornadoes and Severe Storms"** ← **chosen**
   It keeps the hook and the scope. "Access to federal … grants" signals that this is a paper about intergovernmental transfers. "County size" (not "capacity") avoids implying a causal claim about capacity.
2. "Who Applies? County Size and the Geography of Federal Hazard Mitigation Grants"
   This is regional and short, but it drops the hazard scope and the "declared" hook, and a question as title is weaker for indexing.
3. "Small Counties and the Application Margin in Intergovernmental Hazard Mitigation Grants"
   This is the most precise about the finding but the dullest; "application margin" is jargon in a title.
4. "Unequal Access to Federal Mitigation Funds: Evidence from County Participation in the Hazard Mitigation Grant Program"
   The framing is clear, but it loses the hook and repeats "mitigation."

The chosen title is used in `main.tex`, the PDF metadata, the cover letter and the author information page. To change it, edit those four places.
